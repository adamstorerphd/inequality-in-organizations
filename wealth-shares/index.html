India and China
Neither government publishes a regular series of wealth shares by group. India’s All-India Debt and Investment Survey and China’s household finance surveys under-count the very rich, so researchers combine them with tax records and billionaire lists. That is what the World Inequality Database figures above do.

Reading the numbers

Wealth is not income

Wealth is what you own minus what you owe, built up over years and passed on. It is always far more concentrated than income. In the United States the richest 10% get about 47% of income but own about 70% of wealth.

A share can be below zero

People whose debts exceed their assets (student loans, mortgages under water) have negative net wealth. That is why Finland’s poorest tenth owns −0.9%, and why the US poorest half owns only a sliver.

People or households?

The World Inequality Database splits wealth equally between adult partners and ranks adults. Statistics Finland and the Federal Reserve rank households. Different units give different shares for the “same” group.

Surveys miss the top

The very rich rarely answer surveys and under-report when they do. Estimates that add tax data and rich lists (WID) usually show a larger top share than survey-only figures. That is one reason Finland’s top 10% is 55.5% in WID and 51.8% in the official survey.
